Comcast

championing diversity, equity, and inclusion through research and strategic initiatives

The Overview

Introduction

At Comcast, I served as the Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion Public Policy Researcher, working to foster an inclusive workplace culture across one of the nation's largest telecommunications companies. My role centered on research, collaboration, and communication by supporting initiatives that aligned with Comcast's broader social impact goals.

This experience focused on organizational culture and policy. I learned that creating inclusive experiences extends beyond user interfaces into the systems, policies, and cultures that shape how people work together. The skills I developed in research synthesis, stakeholder communication, and metrics tracking proved just as essential to UX work as prototyping and wireframing.

Context and Challenge

Background

Comcast's commitment to diversity, equity, and inclusion spans recruitment, retention, advancement, and community engagement. The DEI team works across departments to implement policies, track progress, and create programs that make Comcast a more inclusive workplace for its thousands of employees.

The Problem

How do you measure inclusion? How do you communicate complex DEI strategies to diverse stakeholders? How do you support policy initiatives with solid research while maintaining employee trust and engagement? These questions required critical thinking, careful research, and collaborative problem-solving.

Goals & Objectives

My objectives focused on supporting the DEI team's broader mission through collaborating across departments to build inclusive culture, conducting research to inform policy decisions, organizing materials to communicate strategies effectively, and providing project management support through metrics tracking and documentation.

Key Projects & Solutions

DEI Policy Research

I conducted research to support policy initiatives, analyzing best practices from other organizations, reviewing academic literature on workplace inclusion, and synthesizing findings into actionable recommendations. This research informed decisions about everything from hiring practices to employee resource group support.

The research process taught me to approach sensitive topics with rigor and empathy. Data could illuminate patterns of inequity, but numbers alone couldn't capture the lived experiences behind them. I learned to combine quantitative metrics with qualitative insights, creating research that was both credible and human-centered.

Strategic Communications & Reporting

I organized internal materials and reports to communicate DEI strategies and progress to stakeholders at all levels. This meant translating complex initiatives into clear narratives, creating presentations that resonated with different audiences, and maintaining documentation that tracked our work over time.

Project Management & Metrics Tracking

I provided project management support by tracking key metrics and maintaining documentation for ongoing initiatives. This involved creating systems to monitor progress toward DEI goals, updating stakeholders on milestones, and ensuring accountability through clear record-keeping.

Metrics tracking revealed the importance of choosing meaningful measures. Not everything that matters can be counted, and not everything that can be counted matters. I learned to balance quantitative indicators (representation numbers, participation rates) with qualitative measures (employee sentiment, cultural shifts) to paint a complete picture of progress.

The Results

Conclusion

My work at Comcast contributed to ongoing DEI initiatives that touched thousands of employees. The research I conducted informed policy recommendations, the materials I organized helped communicate strategies across the organization, and the metrics I tracked provided visibility into progress toward inclusion goals.

I learned that metrics tell stories, but the way we choose, frame, and act on those metrics reflects our values. Numbers can reveal disparities, but addressing them requires organizational commitment, cultural change, and sustained effort. Progress isn't linear, and meaningful change takes time.
